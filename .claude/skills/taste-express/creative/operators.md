# Creative operators

Moves that transform a brief fragment into an idea. `scripts/draw.py` picks some at random; you apply them to the fragments you mined. None of them implies a style.

- **Literalize**: take an expression, idiom or word from the brief literally and stage it. ("Mettre de côté": things physically pushed to the side of the frame.)
- **Invert**: show the opposite, the absence, the "before", or what is lost without the product.
- **Rescale**: make the tiny monumental or the huge minuscule.
- **Point of view**: tell it from the object's, the number's, the place's or a bystander's point of view; from above, from inside, from underneath.
- **Personify**: give the product, the number or the problem a character, a voice, a face made of its own parts.
- **Collide**: fuse the brand's truth with a drawn foreign domain until a third thing appears.
- **Transplant the medium**: the message lives on an object from another context (a label, a sign, a certificate, a menu, a form, a score sheet…) taken from the drawn domains.
- **Subtract**: remove the most obvious element (the product, the photo, the headline, the logo) and keep the meaning.
- **Exaggerate**: push one true fact to an absurd extreme.
- **Juxtapose**: two things side by side whose gap makes the point.
- **Sequence**: show time passing (stages, frames, growth, decay, a countdown).
- **Diagram**: explain it as a system, map, cross-section, or chart borrowed from a drawn domain.
- **Type as image**: the words themselves draw the picture.
- **Hide and reveal**: part is hidden (folded, covered, cropped, behind the swipe) and discovered.
- **Repeat**: repeat one element until the repetition forms a new shape or a new meaning.
- **Wrong material**: render the subject in a material that does not belong to it.
- **Borrow a cultural format**: a well-known format (a film poster, a weather report, a recipe, a match report, a horoscope…) used without copying any real brand.
- **Question**: the piece asks; the viewer's mind answers.
- **Data made physical**: numbers become quantities of objects you can count, stack, pour, measure.
- **Metaphor chain**: A is like B, so show only B, and let A be understood.
- **Anchor locally**: a real place, time, habit or ritual of the audience, precise enough to be recognised.
- **Twist**: set up an expectation in the first read and break it in the second.
- **Use the format**: make the frame itself the mechanism (the crop, the grid tile, the swipe, the square, the vertical scroll).
- **Accumulate**: many small things adding up to something big.
- **Zoom**: start on a macro detail that reveals the whole, or pull back from the whole to a detail.
- **Found object**: make it feel like something found, used, annotated, handled (never imitating a real organisation's document).
- **Game**: turn it into a puzzle, a maze, a checklist, a spot-the-difference, a count, a scratch card.
- **Instruction**: ask the viewer to do something (count, tilt the phone, hold the thumb, look again).
- **Tension**: stage a contrast (calm/chaos, old/new, heavy/light, loud/quiet) that is the product's own tension.
- **Constraint as style**: a self-imposed rule (one shape, one word, one colour, one line) carried to the end.
- **Mistake**: build on something going wrong (a misprint, a spill, a wrong turn) that the product fixes.
- **Ritual**: show the tiny ritual the audience repeats every day, and where the product slips into it.
